# Daily Work-From-Home Mobility Routine

**Purpose:** a practical, pain-free 14-minute movement break for general mobility,
climbing preparation (shoulders, thoracic spine, wrists, hips, and active
compression), and running preparation (hips, ankles, calves, and single-leg
control). It includes gentle abdominal "compression" work: a controlled exhale
with the lower ribs moving down and in, rather than hard bracing or breath
holding.

**Equipment:** mat; one light-to-moderate kettlebell for the halo. Select a load that
you can move smoothly with no shoulder, wrist, or neck discomfort. If unsure, do the
no-weight option. No chair or wall is needed.

**Effort:** RPE 2-4/10. This is mobility practice, not a conditioning circuit. Move
slowly enough to breathe through your nose and keep all ranges comfortable.

## The 14-minute routine

Set one 14-minute timer and move to the next exercise at each timestamp.

| Time | Movement | Technique cues | Easier / harder | Watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00-1:00 | **Supine 90/90 breathing** | Lie on the mat with hips and knees bent about 90 degrees, feet on the floor. Inhale quietly through the nose. On a 4-6 second exhale, let the lower ribs soften down and gently narrow the lower abdomen; keep the jaw, neck, and glutes relaxed. | **Easier:** keep the knees farther away. **Harder:** lift feet so shins are parallel to the floor, without arching the back. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=90%2F90+breathing+exercise+tutorial) |
| 1:00-2:00 | **Cat-cow with side reach** | From hands and knees, alternate a comfortable spinal rounding and arch. On every other rep, sit hips slightly toward heels and reach one arm long to the opposite side to open the ribs. | **Easier:** use a smaller range. **Harder:** pause for one breath in each end position. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=cat+cow+with+thread+the+needle+side+reach+tutorial) |
| 2:00-3:00 | **Quadruped thoracic rotations** | Keep one hand planted under the shoulder. Put the other hand behind the head, rotate the elbow toward the planted arm, then open toward the ceiling without forcing the low back. Do 30 seconds each side. | **Easier:** rotate only as far as the elbow moves freely. **Harder:** reach the top arm toward the ceiling at the open position. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=quadruped+thoracic+rotation+open+book+tutorial) |
| 3:00-4:00 | **Shoulder controlled circles** | Stand tall. Make one slow, pain-free arm circle at a time: thumb leads behind, palm turns out, then the arm returns in front. Keep ribs stacked over the pelvis; do 30 seconds each arm. | **Easier:** bend the elbow or use a smaller circle. **Harder:** use the fullest smooth circle available, not a forced stretch. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=controlled+arm+circles+shoulder+mobility+tutorial) |
| 4:00-5:00 | **Wrist rocks and finger pulses** | On hands and knees, hands under shoulders, gently shift forward and back. Turn fingers slightly outward only if comfortable; finish with relaxed finger opens and closes. Keep pressure spread through the whole hand. | **Easier:** make the shift smaller or do it from fists. **Harder:** turn fingers toward knees for a few small, controlled rocks, only if the wrists tolerate it. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=wrist+mobility+rocks+climbers+tutorial) |
| 5:00-6:00 | **Kettlebell halo** | Hold the horns close to the chest. Circle the bell slowly around the head, keeping it close and the ribs from flaring. Change direction at 30 seconds. | **Easier:** make a large arm circle with no weight. **Harder:** use a slightly heavier bell only if the neck and shoulders stay quiet. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=kettlebell+halo+tutorial+technique) |
| 6:00-7:00 | **Deep-squat shifts** | Take a comfortable wide stance, descend only as far as the heels stay down, and shift gently side to side. Keep the chest long and use the elbows to make room, not to force the knees. | **Easier:** squat higher or hold the kettlebell lightly at the chest as a counterweight. **Harder:** pause over each ankle and add a small, controlled hip shift. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=deep+squat+hip+shifts+mobility+tutorial) |
| 7:00-8:00 | **Cossack squats** | Step wide, bend one knee while the other leg lengthens with the foot planted or toes up, then return through center. Alternate sides; keep the bent-knee foot and knee tracking in the same direction. | **Easier:** keep the hips higher and use a shallow range. **Harder:** reach the straight-leg toes up and pause briefly at the bottom. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=cossack+squat+tutorial+form) |
| 8:00-9:00 | **Spiderman with thoracic reach** | From a plank or hands-and-knees position, step one foot outside the same-side hand. Sink only into a mild hip and inner-thigh stretch, then rotate that side's arm toward the ceiling. Alternate sides. | **Easier:** lower the back knee. **Harder:** keep the back knee lifted and reach the opposite arm forward before rotating. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=spiderman+stretch+with+thoracic+rotation+tutorial) |
| 9:00-10:30 | **Half-kneeling lunge to hamstring flow** | From a half-kneeling lunge, gently tuck the pelvis and shift forward, then move the hips back and straighten the front leg for a mild hamstring stretch. Alternate sides at 45 seconds; keep the spine long rather than reaching for the toes. | **Easier:** keep the front knee bent and use a smaller shift. **Harder:** add the same-side arm reach during the lunge and a 1-2 second pause in each end position. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=half+kneeling+lunge+hip+flexor+to+hamstring+stretch+tutorial) |
| 10:30-11:30 | **Glute bridge** | Lie on your back with feet under the knees. Exhale, gently tuck the pelvis, lift until the hips form a comfortable line with the ribs, pause, and lower slowly. Do not arch to gain height. | **Easier:** use a smaller lift or hold the top. **Harder:** alternate a slow heel lift or march only if the pelvis stays level. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=glute+bridge+tutorial+form) |
| 11:30-12:30 | **Slow calf raises and ankle rocks** | Rise onto both forefeet for two seconds, pause, and lower for three seconds. Then spend the remaining time alternating a small standing ankle drive with the heel heavy. Keep weight through the big-toe base and second toe. | **Easier:** reduce the height and range. **Harder:** use single-leg calf raises or a slightly deeper ankle drive while keeping the arch controlled. | [Search demo](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=slow+calf+raise+ankle+rock+mobility+tutorial) |
| 12:30-14:00 | **Dead bug plus seated active compression** | For 45 seconds, alternate dead-bug arm/leg lowers with an exhale and a quiet low back. Then sit tall in a comfortable pike or straddle: brace lightly, lift one heel or leg without leaning back, and hold 10 seconds before switching; use the hands beside the knees for support and keep breathing. Repeat for 45 seconds. | **Easier:** keep one foot down for the dead bug and use bent knees or lift only the heel for compression. **Harder:** lengthen the dead-bug limbs or lift both heels briefly, without rounding aggressively or holding the breath. | [Dead bug](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=dead+bug+exercise+tutorial) / [Pike-straddle compression](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=active+pike+compression+calisthenics+tutorial) |

The timestamps total **14:00 exactly**: 6:00 of preparation, 6:30 of hips,
ankles, and posterior-chain mobility, and 1:30 of trunk/compression work.

## What changed from the image-based routine

- **Kept and made specific:** active pike/straddle compression (10-second holds)
  supports climbing body tension and hip-flexor control; Cossack squats, deep-squat
  shifts, and the Spiderman reach combine adductor, hip, and thoracic movement useful
  for climbing positions and running stride mechanics. The lunge-to-hamstring flow is
  more useful than a passive hamstring stretch because it rehearses controlled
  transitions. Glute bridges add low-cost posterior-chain work for running and
  climbing without making the routine conditioning.
- **Modified:** the rear-foot-elevated quad/hip-flexor stretch is replaced by a
  floor-based half-kneeling lunge flow, which preserves the hip-flexor emphasis
  without a chair or other elevation. The full bridge is replaced by a glute bridge:
  it supplies hip extension with less end-range spinal, shoulder, and wrist loading
  for a daily routine.
- **Not added as separate blocks:** the active compression, deep squat, Cossack, and
  Spiderman work cover the useful hip/adductor overlap, so adding every image
  movement would make the session longer and more fatiguing. Full bridge and
  rear-foot elevation are the more demanding/riskier choices when performed cold or
  with limited shoulder, wrist, knee, or lumbar tolerance.

## Simple weekly variation

Use the routine on workdays, before an easy run, or after a climbing session at the
same easy effort. It should leave you looser or unchanged, not fatigued.

| Days | Version | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday, Wednesday, Friday | **A: base routine** | Do the 14 minutes above. |
| Tuesday, Thursday | **B: climbing emphasis** | At 6:00-7:00, make the deep-squat shifts shallower and spend more time on controlled Cossack range. At 4:00-5:00, keep wrist rocks especially gentle. |
| Saturday or Sunday, if desired | **C: running emphasis** | Keep the base routine, but use the harder calf-raise option and a smaller, smoother Cossack range. Skip the harder options if the calves or feet feel worked from running. |
| At least one day per week | **Off or easy walk** | Take the day off from the routine if it feels like another obligation; a short walk and normal movement breaks are enough. |

## How to fit it around training

- **Before a run or climb:** use the routine as written, keeping the kettlebell halo
  and compression especially easy. It is a preparation tool, not a warm-up
  replacement for a hard session; add sport-specific gradual warm-up afterwards.
- **After climbing:** use the no-weight halo or omit it if the forearms, shoulders, or
  elbows are tired. Keep wrist work and compression gentle rather than stretching or
  gripping aggressively.
- **After running:** prioritize the hip, ankle, calf, and dead-bug portions. Do not
  push range to chase tightness after a hard or long run.

## Safety and self-checks

- Keep every movement pain-free. Mild muscular effort or a gentle stretch is
  acceptable; sharp pain, pinching, numbness, tingling, instability, altered gait,
  or a worsening symptom is a stop signal for that movement.
- Do not force joint range, bounce, hold the breath, or use the kettlebell where you
  cannot control it close to the body. Mobility varies from day to day. Stay within a
  comfortable depth on squat, Cossack, Spiderman, lunge, and bridge variations.
- Stop exercising and seek urgent medical assessment for chest pain, fainting, severe
  breathlessness, or other concerning symptoms. For persistent or worsening joint,
  tendon, or nerve symptoms, seek assessment from an appropriate clinician.
- Progress by improving control and breathing first. Add range, time, or kettlebell
  load only one at a time and only when the current version feels comfortable for
  1-2 weeks.
